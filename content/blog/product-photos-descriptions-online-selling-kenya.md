Online shoppers cannot touch fabric, test a hinge, compare an item against their room or ask a shelf attendant an immediate question. Product content has to perform those jobs. When photographs hide scale and descriptions repeat vague supplier language, buyers hesitate, open WhatsApp for clarification or order with the wrong expectation. The cost appears later as abandoned carts, support work, exchanges and returns.

This guide gives Kenyan online sellers a repeatable way to produce accurate photographs and descriptions without needing a large studio. It focuses on decision-making information, mobile performance and catalogue operations. ## Begin with the customer’s unanswered questions

Before taking a photograph, list what a careful buyer needs to know. What exactly is included? How large is it? What material is it made from? Which device or use is it compatible with? How does the colour look in ordinary light? What changes between variants? What maintenance does it require? What happens if it does not fit?

Review actual messages, calls, returns and in-store questions. Group them by product family. These are stronger content prompts than generic keyword lists because they expose the uncertainty blocking a purchase. A customer asking “does it come with the charger?” is telling you the page has failed to define the package.

Turn repeated questions into structured fields. Clothing might need garment measurements, model measurements, fit, stretch, fabric and care. Electronics might need model number, ports, power, compatibility, warranty and box contents. Furniture might need width, depth, height, material, assembly and delivery constraints. Structure makes content easier to compare, filter and maintain.

## Create a minimum photo set for every product

Use a consistent shot list so the catalogue does not depend on inspiration. A useful minimum is a clean primary image, front and back or multiple angles, a close detail, a scale or in-use image, package contents and a photograph of any important label, texture or interface.

The main image should identify the exact product quickly. Use a simple background and enough contrast around the edges. Do not include accessories that are not supplied unless the caption makes that clear. If several variants look similar, photograph the differences and label them correctly rather than reusing one image across every SKU.

Scale is critical. Show the item beside a familiar object, on a person with stated measurements or inside the context where it will be used. Avoid wide-angle distortion that makes a room item look larger. For small products, include dimensions in text rather than relying only on a ruler inside an image.

## Build an affordable, consistent photo setup

A modern phone, stable support and controlled light can produce useful commerce images. Choose a space near indirect daylight or use two diffused lights. Keep the background, camera height and distance consistent. Lock focus and exposure where possible, clean the lens and product, and capture the full shot list in one session.

Consistency matters more than decorative styling. If every primary image uses a different crop or colour cast, comparison becomes harder. Mark a shooting area, create a product-position guide and save one editing preset that corrects exposure and white balance without changing the product’s real colour.

Photograph a colour reference when accurate colour is essential, and inspect results on more than one ordinary phone. Screens vary, so avoid absolute colour promises. State where appearance may differ because of lighting or display. Never retouch away seams, surface variation or details a customer reasonably expects to see.

## Name and organise image files

Use a product identifier in file names and folders. A pattern such as `sku-angle-version` makes it easier to match assets, identify replacements and avoid uploading the wrong colour. Keep the high-resolution original separately from web-ready exports.

Define who approves an image and what makes it publishable. Confirm it matches the SKU, represents included items honestly, uses the correct crop and does not expose personal or sensitive information. Record the version when suppliers change packaging or specifications.

At scale, store the connection between product, variant and asset in the catalogue system rather than relying on staff memory. Clear identifiers are also essential when synchronising products across a website, POS or marketplace. ## Optimise images for mobile shoppers

Uploading the largest phone photo directly to a store wastes data and slows the page. Export images near the maximum dimensions in which they will be displayed, use an efficient web format supported by the platform and retain enough quality for the product detail to remain clear. A thumbnail does not need the same file as a zoomable product view.

Use responsive images so the browser can select an appropriate size. Reserve the image’s dimensions in the layout to prevent content jumping while it loads. Load the first important product image promptly, then defer images lower on the page. Measure on a mid-range phone and realistic connection, not only on office fibre.

Compression should not destroy evidence. Jewellery detail, fabric texture or small printed specifications may need a sharper export than a simple lifestyle photograph. Create a standard target, then allow justified exceptions. ## Write titles that identify the product

A product title should help a buyer and internal team distinguish the item. Include the product type, meaningful differentiator and relevant size, capacity or model where it helps. Avoid stuffing every search phrase into the title or adding promotional claims that belong elsewhere.

“Bottle” is too vague. “750 ml stainless-steel insulated bottle” is identifiable. A fashion title might combine garment type, defining cut and collection name. An electronics title should use the exact model where compatibility depends on it. Apply the same naming rule across the catalogue.

Do not put temporary stock or delivery claims in the title. Those change independently and should come from their own fields. Stable product names produce cleaner URLs, reports and integrations.

## Use descriptions to support a decision

Open with two or three sentences explaining who the item is for, what problem it solves and its most important limitation or distinction. Follow with scannable facts: materials, dimensions, box contents, compatibility, care, warranty and origin where verified. Then explain delivery or installation considerations that are specific to the product.

Replace adjectives with evidence. Instead of “high quality,” describe the material, construction or warranty. Instead of “perfect for everyone,” identify the situation where it works well. Do not copy a supplier description without verifying it against the exact stock received. Suppliers can change packaging and specifications.

Use familiar units and define how measurements were taken. For garments, separate body measurements from garment measurements. For accessories, list compatible model numbers rather than saying “universal” unless you have verified that claim. A clear limitation can increase trust because it prevents the wrong buyer from ordering.

## Treat variants as real data

Size, colour, capacity and pack quantity are not free-form notes. Give each sellable variant a unique SKU, price, availability and relevant image. Make the selection state visible before the buyer adds to cart. Do not allow a default variant to create accidental purchases.

If the appearance changes materially, show variant-specific photos. If only pack quantity changes, make the price basis clear. Retired variants should not remain selectable because a stale page or marketplace feed still references them.

Variant discipline reduces overselling and fulfilment mistakes. It also makes performance reporting useful: you can see that medium sells while large is accumulating rather than treating one parent product as the whole story.

## Design size and fit information for Kenya

International size labels are inconsistent, and local brands may use their own cuts. Publish measurements for the actual garment or product batch. Explain fit, stretch and model context. Tell buyers how to measure themselves with a short diagram or text guide.

Do not hide the chart in a blurry image. Use accessible text or a table that works on mobile and can be updated. If one product runs differently, show product-specific guidance rather than relying on a sitewide chart. The article on [custom size guides for Nairobi fashion brands](/blog/custom-size-guide-ecommerce-ux) explores interactive recommendations and the data they require.

Track exchange reasons by product and size. Content should improve from this evidence. If buyers repeatedly say a shoe runs small, verify the pattern and add precise guidance instead of leaving support to repeat the warning privately.

## Add trust information beside the product

Shoppers need to know who is selling, whether the displayed price is current, how delivery works and what happens if the item arrives wrong or damaged. Link concise policy summaries near the buying decision and keep the full policy easy to reach. For higher-risk items, explain warranty ownership and the claim process.

Use genuine reviews with permission and connect them to the correct product. A useful review discusses fit, use, delivery or durability; a collection of anonymous superlatives looks less credible. Answer product questions publicly when they are broadly useful, without exposing customer information.

## Make content accessible and searchable

Alternative text should convey the meaningful visual information for someone who cannot see the image. “Black 750 ml bottle shown with lid and carry loop” is more useful than a stuffed string of keywords. Decorative images can use empty alternative text where the platform supports it.

Use one clear product heading and logical subheadings. Keep specifications as real text. Add captions where a photograph needs context, such as identifying an optional accessory. Ensure controls for galleries and variants work by keyboard and have understandable labels.

Search visibility follows product clarity. Use the language buyers use naturally, but write for the purchasing decision. A unique, accurate description has more value than a hundred manufacturer paragraphs repeated across competing shops.

## Create a catalogue production workflow

Define stages such as received, identified, measured, photographed, edited, written, verified, published and reviewed. Assign an owner and acceptance check to each. No product should publish until price, SKU, stock state, variant mapping, delivery constraints and return treatment are known.

Use a template for each product family, but do not turn it into filler. Required fields ensure coverage; the actual words and photographs must describe the item. Keep a change log for material specification, price or packaging updates. Schedule reviews for important and frequently changing products.

When a catalogue grows, manual copying creates inconsistent titles and stock across channels. Connect the authoritative product record to the storefront and other channels, validate required fields and report failed updates. ## Measure whether product content works

Track product-page visits, add-to-cart, completed orders, product questions, returns and exchange reasons. A high question rate can indicate missing information. A high add-to-cart rate followed by checkout abandonment may point to price, delivery or payment rather than content. A high conversion rate with high returns suggests the page is persuasive but inaccurate.

Test meaningful changes, not decorative churn. Add a scale photo, clarify box contents, replace an inaccurate colour image or improve sizing guidance. Compare a sufficient period while accounting for campaigns and stock changes. Keep what helps buyers make a correct decision.

The best product page does not pressure everyone to buy. It helps the right customer understand the item, cost and next step, while giving the wrong customer enough information to avoid a mistaken order. That accuracy supports conversion, trust and healthier operations at the same time.
